# Related work and MindCommons' proposed role

Many people and groups are already working on data rights, privacy, community governance, and AI. MindCommons does not claim to be the first AI cooperative. This comparison is a short research snapshot, checked November 19, 2026.

It explores a small agreement that connects group rules, each person's permission, a specific task, and benefit conditions.                                                                  |

## What MindCommons is testing

The prototype tests one narrow idea: a participating AI service can read a shared, machine-readable agreement and use fixed software checks to decide whether a specific request follows it. In the demo, the checks consider the current community rule, each relevant contributor's permission, the requested purpose and task, limits on the model and output, and any required benefit terms.

The software cannot make a community's decision legitimate. It cannot force an outside service to follow the agreement, control copied data or model weights, or take back results that have already been shared. It does not prove that the approach is legally sufficient or that communities want this tool.

## Questions for future co-design

If a host relationship is established, people affected should decide whether this approach is useful, which rules matter, what alternatives are needed, and how authority, appeals, safety, language, compensation, and accountability should work. They may choose a different direction. No SRHIN integration, endorsement, or community feedback is claimed.
