# Who gets to make the rules?

MindCommons can show software checking a recorded community rule. It cannot prove that the person who entered the rule speaks for a real community or that the decision was fair.

The demo uses one fictional community representative and a pre-set scenario. The role switcher is only a way to explore the software. It does not check a person's identity, mandate or authority.

## Questions people would need to decide together

If an authorized host relationship is established, the people affected should decide how governance works. Possible options include:

| Question                            | Examples to discuss                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who can propose a rule?             | Any affected person, a selected group, or a joint working group.                                 |
| Who reviews and decides?            | Delegates with a clear mandate, a community meeting, affected groups, or more than one body.     |
| How are representatives chosen?     | A community-defined process, rotation, election if wanted, or a trusted local process.           |
| How can someone object?             | A private route, a supported advocate, a group review, or a time-limited objection period.       |
| How are disagreements handled?      | Facilitated discussion, a community-chosen panel, an appeal, or an outside safeguarding contact. |
| Who can pause a study?              | Affected contributors, community delegates, a safety lead, or a shared stop process.             |
| When are rules reviewed?            | On a schedule, when a model or purpose changes, or when a contributor asks.                      |
| How can benefits be changed?        | Joint renegotiation, community review, or escalation when a promise is late.                     |
| How are representatives answerable? | Public reasons, term limits, conflict disclosures, replacement, or independent review.           |

These are examples, not a recommended system. A simple majority vote should not automatically be treated as legitimate. People should be able to raise concerns without losing unrelated services or facing retaliation.

## What the software currently does

The demo can store versions of a policy and check the active version before a fixed synthetic task. It does not implement real representative selection, voting standards, objections, appeals, safeguarding, or authority to pause a real study.

No community has approved this design. If a fellowship proceeds, people affected must be able to change or reject these assumptions. See the [decisions reserved for co-design](../fellowship/community-decisions-after-selection.md) and [proposed roadmap](../fellowship/srhin-embedded-roadmap.md).
