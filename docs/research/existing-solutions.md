This is a research snapshot, not a claim of novelty. Updated 2026-11-19 after reviewing the Wellcome GENSCORE award record and SRHIN's lived-experience AI co-design guide.

## Relevant Nigerian community-health context

SRHIN's public article on its BraveHeart Innovation describes community leaders trained to recognize anxiety and depression and provide psychosocial support and mental-health education to young people in two IDP camps. This is useful local context for designing with community health workers and young people. It is not a MindCommons pilot, endorsement, partnership, or evidence that SRHIN has agreed to host this project. Source: <https://srhin.org/breaking-the-silence-how-braveheart-innovation-is-bringing-mental-health-closer-to-young-people-in-abujas-idp-camps/>.

### GENSCORE and lived-experience co-design

Wellcome's 2026 [GENSCORE grant record](https://wellcome.org/research-funding/funding-portfolio/funded-grants/genscore-project-leveraging-generative-ai-adapt) names SRHIN staff among the grant holders. It describes a project aiming to improve culturally grounded depression and anxiety screening in Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa and Yoruba. The record says the project plans to co-develop a dataset and model, co-design screening tools with people with lived experience and mental-health experts, and validate them with a planned 990 participants. These are project aims and planned work in the grant summary, not results established by the record. It also explicitly describes ethical, governance, safety and data-sovereignty frameworks, so MindCommons should not imply that SRHIN or GENSCORE has no governance work.

SRHIN's [A Basic Guide for People with Lived Experience to Get Involved in AI Mental Health Tool Design](https://resources.srhin.org/item/ai-co-design-guide) is an eight-page, plain-language guide from its Lived Experience Co-Design Team, drawn from GENSCORE and published under CC BY 4.0. It is existing public work on involving people with lived experience in AI mental-health tool design. It is not a MindCommons collaboration, endorsement or evidence that the MindCommons governance mechanism has been adopted.

The GENSCORE project and guide materially narrow MindCommons' proposed distinction: the prototype should be presented as an experiment in portable, enforceable rules for participating services, including individual permissions, approved purposes and benefit obligations. The guide raises concrete questions about what people contribute, recording and transcription, how contributions are used in training, and how contributors can receive feedback about their use. These are useful design questions, not proof that GENSCORE's own processes are inadequate. MindCommons should complement, not claim to invent or replace, lived-experience co-design, clinical research, or GENSCORE's stated ethics and governance work. Any technical relationship or integration would need the host's and relevant communities' direction; none exists now.

## What MindCommons proposes to add

An open, independently deployable reference agreement and evaluator linking a versioned community rule, separate individual permissions, bounded job and model restrictions, authorization, output-release recheck, provenance, and benefit obligations. MindCommons uses the evaluator; a separate minimal Worker example demonstrates how another participating service can consume the same agreement. This is an interoperability experiment with a concrete scope, not a claim of novelty or a universal enforcement protocol. It cannot control unrestricted copies of datasets or model weights, services that ignore the agreement, or downstream use of already released outputs. The prototype does not prove legal compliance, consent validity, model unlearning, privacy-preserving computation, representative governance, or adoption.

## Further validation needed

Test whether the agreement schema is understandable and useful to independent researchers and implementers, compare it with existing licences and data-governance standards, and seek input from Nigeria-based community health practitioners. Do not imply that this separate example is deployed or interoperates with Mozilla Data Collective, PySyft, or Flower. Seek no data or endorsement from SRHIN unless a real relationship is established. Co-design authority, appeals, safety, language, compensation, and accountability arrangements before any pilot.
