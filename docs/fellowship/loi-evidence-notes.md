# Notes for the applicant's Letter of Intent

These are facts and sources for the applicant to consider. They are not draft answers. The applicant must choose the argument, examples, wording and disclosure of AI assistance in their own voice.

**Sources checked:** November 19, 2026.

## What the public sources say

Mozilla's SRHIN host brief describes a concern that information from African communities may be used to develop AI without enough say over its use or benefits. It proposes exploring community-led ways to govern data and AI, explain choices, protect privacy and share benefits. Potential future work could involve young people, people with lived experience and community health workers. This is the host's published challenge. It is not an independent estimate of how common the problem is, and it is not an endorsement of MindCommons.

Mozilla asks applicants to explain who needs the work and how they know. The project currently has no community users, feedback or evidence of demand. The fellowship may allow an early-stage idea, but that does not turn this missing evidence into proof. Future, authorized co-design would need to explore the need.

SRHIN has published community mental-health work. Its 2024 Brave Heart study reports that 100 young people displaced in Northern Nigeria enrolled and 83 completed the follow-up; 20 community members were trained. The study used one group before and after an intervention. It can describe SRHIN's community-delivered research experience, but it does not prove that the intervention caused the observed changes or that people want MindCommons. SRHIN's 2026 BraveHeart Innovation account is the organization's own report, not an independent evaluation.

Wellcome's [2026 GENSCORE grant record](https://wellcome.org/research-funding/funding-portfolio/funded-grants/genscore-project-leveraging-generative-ai-adapt) lists SRHIN staff among the grant holders. It describes plans for mental-health AI in Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa and Yoruba, work with people with lived experience and mental-health experts, and proposed community and clinical validation. These are planned aims, not completed results shown by the record. It also names ethics, governance, safety and data sovereignty as part of the intended work. Do not suggest SRHIN or GENSCORE lacks community work or governance.

SRHIN's [public guide for people with lived experience](https://resources.srhin.org/item/ai-co-design-guide) explains ways people can help shape mental-health AI tools. It discusses creating expressions, reviewing transcripts, testing tools, and asking how a contribution is used. The guide makes explanation, support and feedback relevant design questions. It is not a MindCommons partnership, endorsement, or proof that MindCommons is needed.

## Facts the applicant should express personally

- Why the problem matters to the applicant, based on their own experience and reasoning.
- What they have personally built or learned, including their actual role and evidence.
- What they hope to learn with a host, and how they would respond if people change or reject the design.
- Accurate status: a public prototype exists, but there are no community users, participant feedback, partnership, pilot or adoption results.
- Eligibility, availability and other details requested by the official form.

## Word limits and disclosure

The official RFP lists a separate word limit for each Letter of Intent question:

| Question                                    |     Limit |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------: |
| Explain the public project or artifact      | 200 words |
| Why this host?                              | 100 words |
| Project summary                             | 200 words |
| One-line project description                |  30 words |
| Who is the project for?                     |  50 words |
| What have you tried so far?                 |  50 words |
| What change do you hope to make?            | 150 words |
| What is different about your approach?      |  50 words |
| What would you do during the fellowship?    | 150 words |
| How would the project benefit the public?   | 150 words |
| Relevant professional experience            | 150 words |
| Technical experience                        | 100 words |
| What do you hope to do next in your career? | 150 words |
| What are your next steps?                   | 150 words |

Check every limit against the live application form before submitting in case the questions have changed. The call lists the deadline as December 17, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. Pacific Standard Time (UTC−8). Its AI policy says submitted responses must represent the applicant's own ideas and voice. Support tools may help with tasks such as clarity or translation, but the applicant should describe any AI assistance accurately. Do not submit these notes as answers.
