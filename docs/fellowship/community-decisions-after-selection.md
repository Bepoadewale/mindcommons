# Decisions to leave open for community co-design

MindCommons is an independent prototype. Writing software or adding fictional roles does not give the developer authority to decide what a real community should accept. A fellowship award would not replace the need for an agreed relationship, authorization and safeguards.

## Questions for people affected to decide

- Who is affected, and who should be invited to shape the process?
- What information, if any, should be considered? What should stay out of scope?
- Which research purposes, AI models, results and later uses are acceptable or not?
- What should people be told before they make a choice? In which languages and formats?
- What should happen when an individual says no but a group approves a project?
- Who may propose, review, approve, reject, pause and revisit rules?
- How should representatives be chosen and held accountable?
- How can someone object, appeal, ask for support or report harm safely?
- If voting is used, what makes the process fair and legitimate beyond a simple majority?
- Which benefits matter, who is responsible for them, and what happens if they are late?
- What rules are needed for storage, deletion, incidents and outside model providers?
- What ethics, legal, privacy, safeguarding and security reviews are needed before any pilot?

## Proposed process

First establish an authorized relationship with a host. Then agree how young people, people with lived experience and community health workers can take part safely and accessibly. Participants should be able to change or reject the current technical design. Use synthetic information unless a specific activity and its data have received the required approvals. Agree on stop conditions and an alternative that does not involve a pilot.

The demo's role switcher, fictional representative, sample proposal and permission buttons are test examples only. They do not answer the questions above. No community feedback, SRHIN endorsement or pilot approval is claimed.
